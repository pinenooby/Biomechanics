Biomechanics

How a day works
  1. Computer: read the emails and accept an order. Every criterion must be met.
  2. Hover over anything to read its name. Hold the scalpel on a lid, an organ or a limb to unfasten it, then drag it out. A body with no SSPG-1 goes dull. Do this anywhere: scrapyard, workshop or warehouse. A running body must be sedated first.
  3. Drag a whole body anywhere: the Workshop table, your bag, the warehouse, or a map tab. Fastened parts go with it. Unfastened parts fall off and stay behind. Drag parts back into a body, then hold the needle on each to fasten it.
  4. Fill the body with SSPG-1 (Synthetic Standard Plasma Generation 1): drag a gray SSPG-1 bag from your bag onto it. Close both lids with the needle, and wake it with adrenaline. Draw SSPG-1 from dead wrecks with the empty syringe: it comes out as a bag in your bag.
  5. Warehouse: bodies and parts kept there never vanish. Pay to expand it. Rare glowing parts turn up in wrecks, and happy clients sometimes send one.
  6. Packaging: move the unit over, drag it into the crate (sized to the body), drag the lid on, stick the shipping label on, then drag the sealed crate onto the truck. The client answers by email.
  7. Repair orders: accept one and the client's dead unit arrives sedated on the packaging dock. Drag it to the workshop, find what is broken with the diagnostic, fix it, then ship it back.
  8. Research orders: a client sends a prototype to the packaging dock. A prototype organ goes into a body like any organ. Fasten it, fill the body with SSPG-1 and wake it, and a data paper prints into your bag. A prototype injection must be dropped on a patient that is awake: it takes effect and prints the paper. Drag the paper onto the Packaging tab, into the envelope, drag the flap on, then the label, then the sealed envelope onto the truck.
  9. Repair orders and bots: Find parts also carries the client's broken unit from the packaging dock to the workshop table, and never brings a spare chassis for a repair. Right-click or tap a running biomecha in the Playground to give it a job. Every job works on every kind of order.
  10. Automating research: Contract accepts the order (and first checks the warehouse for a ready unit, taking it to the table). Find parts brings the prototype to the bag along with the kit. Assemble fits the prototype organ in the unit. Suture fastens, wakes it and injects an injection prototype. Ship waits until a data paper exists, then sends the envelope and stores the tested unit in the warehouse. Press the star on a unit (warehouse or table) to keep bots from taking it. Any unit can do any job. Execute once does a job one time; Automate keeps doing it at every commission. The Pause work / Resume work bar (shown on every tab while bots work) freezes and restarts everyone, or click a working bot for Pause / Resume on just that one. While a bot works in the place you are watching, a robot arm reaches in with the right tool (needle, scalpel, adrenaline syringe, or a bare hand), and everything the bot moves glides to its new spot. Check and Assemble lift the lids off into the bag so the body is open, fit the organs first, then put the lids back last.
  11. On a phone: touch and hold a body for a moment, then drag. Tap anything to read its name in the status line. The round buttons at the right edge scroll the page.
  12. Playground: wake a finished biomecha, then drag it onto the Playground tab. Click it to ask what to do: walk, chat, fight, or work in the scrapyard as a moving shadow. Faster Athletics means faster work.
  13. Part looks: once a part is scanned, its design shows its strongest stat. Chevrons are Athletics, circuit lines Logics, claw marks Combat, a heart Companion, bands Muscleman. Average parts stay plain, and the rare glowing parts are gold.
  14. End the day on the Computer. Rent is due each night and changes from day to day.
